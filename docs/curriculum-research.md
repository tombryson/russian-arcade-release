# Russian proficiency requirements and assessment policy

Research reviewed on 23 September 2026. This document distinguishes published TORFL requirements from Russian Arcade's teaching and progression rules.

The [curriculum and assessment implementation plan](curriculum-uplift-plan.md) turns these findings into staged engineering, content, validation and migration work. It distinguishes the shipped reference from the full course still to build.

## Conclusions

The existing 50 topics remain useful. They organise teaching, but they do not describe a complete proficiency level. A café can support a simple A1 order or a B2 complaint. The language required, the support provided and the learner's response determine the demand.

The application therefore keeps four separate concepts:

| Concept | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Topic band | Where a subject enters our teaching sequence. |
| Task level | The language and communication required by a particular activity. |
| Word and form difficulty | The existing lexical selection and filtering estimates. |
| Assessed requirement | A specific capability demonstrated by an identifiable response. |

Coins reward participation and buy optional games. Elo estimates performance in a skill. Neither establishes proficiency. Journey milestones organise the story and have their own assessments. Free practice remains available at the learner's chosen level.

## Sources and their limits

The main references are the detailed standards and requirements currently linked by the [Moscow State University testing centre](https://test.irlc.msu.ru/podgotovka-k-testirovaniuu/). Their upload dates are not their publication dates. These are the editions inspected:

| --- | --- | --- |
| [A1 standard](https://test.irlc.msu.ru/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/A1_standart.pdf) | Second edition, 2001; title page identifies it as a draft | 7–10 communication and skills; 11–16 language content |

These are strong sources for a reference syllabus. They are not evidence that every current provider uses identical test forms, timings or scoring. Later editions exist. That study concerns a university entrance interview, not a replacement for the general TORFL exam.

No exam passages, answer banks or textbook chapters have been copied into the application. Our requirement labels and observable criteria are original authoring specifications. Their source references identify the relevant linguistic scope; they do not imply that a university has approved our tasks.

## Language progression

Levels develop earlier language rather than replacing it. Case functions need separate targets. Knowing the ending in one phrase does not demonstrate every use of that case.

| Area | A1 foundation | A2 development | B1 development | B2 development |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nouns and cases | Gender, number, animacy; selected meanings of all six cases | More quantities, time relations, states, destinations and government | Broader semantic functions and noun-phrase control | Finer meanings, competing constructions and register |
| Agreement | Basic adjective and pronoun agreement; introductory declension | Fuller case agreement and common comparisons | Short forms, comparisons and more complex reference | Precise reference and stylistic alternatives |
| Verbs | Conjugation, tense, aspect, requests and commands | Wider verb groups, reflexive contrasts and aspect choices | Mood, aspect, voice and connected narration | Aspect and voice choices across discourse and register |
| Motion | Directed and habitual movement; basic по-/при- forms | Further movement pairs and common arrival, departure and boundary prefixes | Wider routes, prefix meanings and aspect contrasts | Precise path, intention, result and contextual interpretation |
| Sentence structure | Statements, questions, negation and selected linked clauses | Purpose, condition, indirect questions and information order | Broader complex clauses, reported speech and compressed constructions | Argument, qualification, transformation and emphasis |

This table condenses the four source inventories above. The [detailed application requirements](curriculum-requirements.md) separate their observable distinctions by level and skill.

Two corrections matter for generation. A1 is not restricted to unprefixed motion verbs. Also, participles and adverbial participles already appear in the inspected B1 requirements. A global rule such as “participles only at B2” is therefore misleading. Recognising a construction and independently producing it must still be assessed separately.

The [Council of Europe's 2020 Companion Volume](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4) distinguishes linguistic range from control. More demanding language may involve more errors. Its phonology scales focus on intelligibility and prosody, rather than eliminating a foreign accent. Our feedback should reflect these distinctions.

## Communication and assessment scale

Every level requires more than grammar selection. The following are concise reference examples, not minimum lengths for ordinary activities.

| Level | Communication demand | Historical assessment-scale examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Exchange essential personal information; understand simple everyday messages; initiate and answer familiar questions. | Reading 250–300 words; a listening monologue 120–150 words with two presentations; writing 7–10 sentences. |
| A2 | Manage routine transactions, explain immediate needs and connect everyday information. | Reading 600–700 words; listening 300–400 words with two presentations; writing 15–18 sentences. |
| B1 | Sustain everyday exchanges, explain attitudes, retell information and produce connected text. | Reading 900–1,000 words; independent writing of at least 20 sentences in the inspected requirements. |
| B2 | Interpret viewpoint and implication; explain and defend a position; adapt language to purpose and recipient. | Reading examples of 300–600 words; listening and speaking tasks involving several minutes of connected language. |

Sources are the skill sections in the A1–B2 references listed above. The shorter B2 reading figure is a useful warning: length alone does not measure difficulty. Text density, inference, register and the question can make a short passage demanding.

Lexical references also exceed our starter lists. The inspected standards give approximately 780 units at A1, 1,300 at A2, 2,300 at B1 and 10,000 at B2, including 6,000 active units at B2. These are cumulative, edition-specific lexical references. RUDN's [current test overview](https://ru.rudn.ru/testing/testirovanie-na-znanie-russkogo-yazyka/) instead lists 760 for A1, illustrating why provenance matters.

The application's 148 A1 target lemmas are introductory course material. They are not the full A1 lexical minimum. Inflected forms must not inflate vocabulary totals. Lexical units can include expressions and distinctions of meaning; a raw database row count is not a proficiency test. The complete published wordlists have not been audited or imported in this pass.

## What assessment must preserve

The A2 and B1 sample rubrics assess communicative purpose, relevant information, coherence and language control. They distinguish errors that alter the message from smaller slips. The B2 sample adds stronger demands on argument, intention, register and spontaneous interaction. Feedback should first describe what the learner communicated, then identify a useful improvement.

Five kinds of evidence must remain distinct:

| Domain | Appropriate evidence | Insufficient substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading | A response that depends on the written source | Recognising one unrelated vocabulary item |
| Listening | Understanding information available in the recording | Reading the transcript, or simply playing audio |
| Language use | Choosing or producing a form for an explicit contextual distinction | A topic tag or a vocabulary count |
| Writing | The learner's own meaningful written response | Selecting a supplied sentence |
| Speaking | The learner's own audible response and interaction | Typed text, repaired captions or a selected reply |

For Russian, record the actual form and context: the governed case, actor, time, aspect, movement or reference being tested. Keep the lemma/form database intact. Meanings remain contextual. A requirement does not add a universal English translation to a lemma.

Speech assessment must preserve the recording and acknowledge unclear evidence. An ASR system may repair a learner's error. The transcript alone must not establish grammatical correctness. A short valid response can achieve a conversational goal without providing enough evidence for an overall fluency judgement.

## Progression decisions

These are application policies, not official TORFL rules.

1. **Practice access stays open.** Prior knowledge does not require replaying the introduction. Learners may choose a harder or easier task.
2. **Practice counts wherever it happened.** The saved activity and response determine eligibility. Entry through Journey is not required.
3. **Preparation is a recommendation.** Ordinary activity practice and focused exercises provide alternative preparation routes. A learner may attempt the current checkpoint early.
4. **A demonstrated target does not require another teaching click.** Correct independent evidence can satisfy preparation without an introduction receipt.
5. **A milestone pass advances the story.** The current letter rubric, component minima, essential answers and support rules remain authoritative for that attempt.
6. **A full-level claim needs all five domains.** Reading choices cannot fill missing writing or speaking evidence. Unsupported or unimplemented assessment remains unmeasured.
7. **Elo does not unlock levels.** It may guide task recommendations. Coins remain a participation and shop mechanism.

For the existing A1 release, preparation uses the greater of activity preparation and focused-target preparation. The activity route retains two successful distinct tasks per topic across two activity families. Focused preparation counts an independently demonstrated target, or an introduced and attempted target. This recommends a checkpoint; it does not award mastery. Supported practice can prepare a learner, but an independent checkpoint pass still has its saved rules.

The policy is named `activity-or-target-practice-v1`. Older A1 releases retain their preparation policy. The published letters, questions, target IDs, audio and scoring remain unchanged. No previous pass is regraded and no earned access is revoked.

## Requirements for a future full-level assessment

The current four letters are a small A1 journey course. They are not a complete A1 proficiency assessment. A broader assessment needs an authored blueprint before becoming a gate:

- Name the exact requirements sampled by each task, its response mode and its evidence criterion.
- Cover all five domains. Keep separate component outcomes so strength in one area cannot hide an untested skill.
- Include original writing and spoken interaction. Use level-appropriate goals, evidence-backed feedback and reviewed acceptable alternatives.
- Record support and uncertainty. Provide a retry for recording or provider failure; do not record that failure as lack of language ability.
- Use equivalent variants with different content. Review ambiguity, distractors, accessibility and answer leakage.
- Compare machine judgements with qualified human marking before using them to restrict progression.

Do not copy a percentage from an exam handbook onto an eight-question game. [Kazan Federal University](https://kpfu.ru/philology-culture/kmlc/exam/trki/o-testirovanii) currently states 66% per subtest. [Tomsk State University](https://test.tsu.ru/ru/trki) publishes a one-subtest exception. The historical A2 sample uses 75%. Provider and edition must remain attached to those rules.

The [Council of Europe's examination-linking guidance](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/relating-examinations-to-the-cefr) and [ALTE assessment guidance](https://www.alte.org/Materials) support explicit specification and validation. A source-backed content map is necessary groundwork; it is not, by itself, evidence that an assessment measures a level reliably.

## Implementation and remaining work

The reference lives in `flask_vocab_app/data/torfl/`. Each requirement has a stable ID, domain, evidence mode, topic relationships and source locator. Levels explicitly build on earlier levels. The read-only loader validates these relationships without changing vocabulary or learner records.

Curriculum displays a closed requirements disclosure for A1–B2. It remains the topic overview, without Journey story cards. Existing activity generators receive at most eight relevant reference criteria. These are planning guidance, not proof of coverage or extra demands on an answer. C1/C2 retain their existing guidance; they are outside this research pass.

The current Journey definitions remain in their frozen catalogue. Aggregate activity grades still establish topic preparation only. They do not silently establish detailed requirements. The remaining assessment work is to author and validate item-level contracts for ordinary activities, expand teaching and lexical coverage, and implement independently assessed writing and speaking in a new versioned level assessment.

This pass does not create A2–B2 Journey chapters, import a licensed lexical minimum or grant TORFL certification. Those are separate content and assessment deliverables. The source map now makes their scope explicit rather than treating another ten topic labels as a complete level.
