Each level builds on the earlier levels. The requirements below are additions or developments. Topic teaching bands and word difficulty remain separate.

## A1

### Language use

Evidence mode: contextual selection.

#### Noun forms

- **Gender, number and animacy.** Choose a noun form that fits whether the context names one or several people, animals or objects; use its grammatical gender rather than English associations. `a1.language.noun-gender-number-animacy` — [§2.2, pp. 11–14][a1-1]

#### Nominative

- **The person doing the action.** Identify the noun that names who performs an action in a short sentence; do not rely only on first-word position. `a1.language.nominative-subject` — [§2.2.2, p. 11][a1-1]
- **Naming a person or object.** Complete a simple identification with the named person or object in the appropriate noun form. `a1.language.nominative-identification` — [§2.2.2, p. 11][a1-1]
- **Addressing someone.** Select the name used to address a person directly, distinguishing it from the object of an action. `a1.language.nominative-address` — [§2.2.2, p. 11][a1-1]
- **Describing who someone is.** Choose a present role or relationship statement with a noun predicate and no unnecessary present-tense form of быть. `a1.language.nominative-role` — [§2.2.2, p. 11][a1-1]
- **Naming an event.** Complete a brief statement identifying an event that happens today or tomorrow. `a1.language.nominative-event` — [§2.2.2, p. 11][a1-1]
- **Saying what is there.** Choose the form naming an available item or place after есть, distinguishing it from an absence construction. `a1.language.nominative-existence` — [§2.2.2, p. 11][a1-1]

#### Genitive

- **Whose object it is.** Identify an owner from a noun phrase and choose the owner form that preserves that relationship. `a1.language.genitive-possession` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **Part of a place or thing.** Choose a noun form identifying the larger place or thing to which a named part belongs. `a1.language.genitive-part-whole` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **Saying something is absent.** Use нет with the missing person or object in a present-time situation. `a1.language.genitive-absence` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **Nouns after small numbers.** Choose the familiar noun form required after two, three, four or five when stating a price or quantity. `a1.language.genitive-quantity` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **The month in a date.** Supply the month form when naming a calendar date. `a1.language.genitive-calendar-month` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **Where someone comes from.** Distinguish a departure point from a destination by selecting из or с and the corresponding place form. `a1.language.genitive-origin` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **Who has something.** Identify the possessor in an у construction while keeping the possessed object as a separate participant. `a1.language.genitive-owner-u` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]

#### Dative

- **Who receives the action.** Choose the person to whom someone writes, calls or gives something when the verb requires the dative. `a1.language.dative-recipient` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **Saying someone’s age.** Choose the personal-pronoun form identifying whose age is given. `a1.language.dative-age` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **Who needs to do something.** Identify who needs to act using a personal pronoun with нужно and an infinitive. `a1.language.dative-need` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **Going to a person.** Use к with a person as the destination, distinguishing that person from a building or point of origin. `a1.language.dative-person-destination` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]

#### Accusative

- **The object of an action.** Select the person or thing directly affected by a familiar verb, accounting for animacy where the form changes. `a1.language.accusative-object` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **Asking and giving names.** Use the fixed naming pattern with the person whose name is requested; distinguish that person from the name supplied. `a1.language.accusative-name-pattern` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **How long an action lasts.** Choose a duration phrase for a stated length of time, distinguishing it from a date or starting time. `a1.language.accusative-duration` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **Going into or to a place.** Choose в or на and a destination form when the context describes movement towards a place. `a1.language.accusative-destination` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]
- **At a time or on a weekday.** Interpret or complete a meeting time using в with a clock hour or weekday. `a1.language.accusative-clock-weekday` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a1-1]

#### Instrumental

- **An activity with заниматься.** Choose the activity form governed by заниматься in a familiar study or leisure context. `a1.language.instrumental-activity` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a1-1]
- **A profession with быть.** Choose the noun predicate used with быть when describing a future profession. `a1.language.instrumental-profession` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a1-1]
- **Doing something with someone.** Identify the companion using с and the appropriate person form; distinguish company from a direct object. `a1.language.instrumental-company` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a1-1]
- **What something comes with.** Choose the form naming an ingredient or accompaniment after с. `a1.language.instrumental-ingredient` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a1-1]

#### Prepositional

- **Talking or thinking about something.** Select the topic form after о for a familiar verb of speaking or thinking. `a1.language.prepositional-topic` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a1-1]
- **Where something is.** Choose a stationary location phrase and distinguish it from movement to that same place. `a1.language.prepositional-location` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a1-1]
- **How someone travels.** Choose the transport form after на without confusing it with the traveller’s destination. `a1.language.prepositional-transport` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a1-1]

#### Pronouns

- **Pronouns in a sentence.** Replace a named person with a personal pronoun while preserving that person’s role in a familiar construction. `a1.language.personal-pronoun-cases` — [§2.2.3, p. 13][a1-1]
- **Questions, ownership and reference.** Select a familiar question, possessive, demonstrative, distributive or negative pronoun that identifies the intended referent. `a1.language.pronoun-reference` — [§2.2.3, p. 13][a1-1]

#### Adjectives

- **Describing a noun.** Match a full adjective to the gender and number of a noun in a nominative description. `a1.language.adjective-agreement` — [§2.2.4, p. 13][a1-1]
- **Recognising adjective case forms.** Recognise agreement in a taught adjective–noun phrase after a familiar verb or preposition; introductory recognition does not establish full declension control. `a1.language.adjective-oblique-recognition` — [§2.2.4, p. 13][a1-1]
- **States and obligations.** Choose a familiar short adjective for a person’s stated condition or obligation. `a1.language.short-adjective-state` — [§2.2.4, p. 13][a1-1]

#### Verb forms

- **Matching a verb to its subject.** Choose the person and number form of a taught first- or second-conjugation verb, including familiar stem alternations. `a1.language.verb-conjugation` — [§2.2.5, p. 13][a1-1]
- **Present, past and future.** Select a verb form consistent with when an event happens, including past gender and a familiar simple or compound future. `a1.language.verb-tense` — [§2.2.5, p. 13][a1-1]
- **An action and its completion.** Distinguish an ongoing or repeated action from a completed result using a taught imperfective–perfective pair. `a1.language.verb-aspect` — [§2.2.5, p. 13][a1-1]
- **Requests and instructions.** Choose a taught imperative appropriate for addressing one familiar person or using polite or plural address. `a1.language.imperative` — [§2.2.5, p. 13][a1-1]
- **Verb and dependent phrase.** Select the complement required by a familiar verb rather than transferring an English preposition or object pattern. `a1.language.verb-government` — [§2.2.5, p. 13][a1-1]

#### Verbs of motion

- **Walking and travelling.** Use context to distinguish a current directed journey from repeated travel, and walking from travel by vehicle. `a1.language.motion-basic-pairs` — [§2.2.5, p. 13][a1-1]
- **Setting off and arriving.** Distinguish departure from arrival with taught по- and при- motion forms; the scene must state the movement stage. `a1.language.motion-departure-arrival` — [§2.2.5, p. 13][a1-1]

#### Numbers

- **Quantity and order.** Distinguish how many items there are from which item comes first or next, using familiar cardinals and nominative ordinals. `a1.language.cardinal-and-ordinal` — [§2.2.6, p. 14][a1-1]

#### Word formation

- **Recognising related words.** Recognise a taught root, ending or derivational change without treating every related word as the same grammatical form. `a1.language.word-structure` — [§2.2.1, p. 11][a1-1]

#### Adverbs

- **Where, when and how.** Choose an adverb that answers the intended question, distinguishing location, destination and origin as well as time and manner. `a1.language.adverb-reference` — [§2.2.7, p. 14][a1-1]

#### Sentence structure

- **Statements, questions and negatives.** Choose a simple sentence that preserves whether a fact is asserted, questioned or denied. `a1.language.sentence-negation` — [§2.3.1, p. 14][a1-1]
- **Conditions and permission.** Interpret a familiar subjectless state or permission/prohibition construction without inventing a personal subject. `a1.language.impersonal-modal` — [§2.2.7, p. 14; §2.3.1–2.3.2, pp. 14–15][a1-1]
- **Joining and contrasting ideas.** Choose a conjunction that expresses addition, contrast or alternatives between two short propositions. `a1.language.coordination` — [§2.3.4, p. 16][a1-1]
- **When and why.** Connect familiar events with a time or reason clause while preserving which event explains the other. `a1.language.time-and-reason-clauses` — [§2.3.4, p. 16][a1-1]
- **What someone says or knows.** Identify the content of a familiar statement or question embedded after a verb of speech or knowledge. `a1.language.complement-clauses` — [§2.3.4, p. 16][a1-1]
- **Which person or object.** Recognise the referent of a short taught relative clause without changing the main sentence’s participants. `a1.language.relative-reference` — [§2.3.4, p. 16][a1-1]
- **Reporting a short message.** Match a short direct message to a simple indirect report that preserves speaker, place and intended information. `a1.language.reported-speech` — [§2.3.5, p. 16][a1-1]
- **Sentence structure.** Recognise ordinary phrase and clause order while using inflections to identify roles; accept equivalent grammatical arrangements. `a1.language.neutral-word-order` — [§2.3.6, p. 16][a1-1]

### Reading

Evidence mode: reading selection.

- **Reading Cyrillic.** Recognise familiar printed Russian words without substituting similarly shaped Latin letters. `a1.reading.cyrillic-decoding` — [§2.1, p. 11][a1-1]
- **Finding practical information.** Find the needed place, person, opening time, item or price in a short everyday written message. `a1.reading.practical-information` — [§1.1.2, p. 7; §1.2.2, p. 9][a1-1]
- **Understanding a short account.** Identify the main event and important details in an unfamiliar short account using language at this level. `a1.reading.narrative-meaning` — [§1.2.2, p. 9][a1-1]
- **Following people and events.** Follow who a pronoun refers to and which event happens before or after another in a simple text. `a1.reading.reference-and-sequence` — [§1.2.2, p. 9; §2.3, pp. 14–16][a1-1]

### Listening

Evidence mode: listening selection.

- **Hearing word differences.** Distinguish taught words when consonant hardness, voicing or stress changes the word heard; no written answer is revealed before listening. `a1.listening.sound-contrasts` — [§2.1, p. 11][a1-1]
- **Understanding a spoken message.** Identify the main information and a requested detail from a short clear recording without a transcript supplying the answer. `a1.listening.short-message` — [§1.2.1 A, p. 8][a1-1]
- **Understanding what someone wants.** Recognise whether a speaker is asking, offering, accepting, refusing or requesting repetition in a short everyday exchange. `a1.listening.dialogue-intention` — [§1.2.1 B, p. 8][a1-1]

### Writing

Evidence mode: independent writing.

- **Writing a personal message.** Write an original brief message with an intelligible purpose, recipient and the requested information; copying a model is not independent evidence. `a1.writing.personal-message` — [§1.2.3, p. 9][a1-1]
- **Describing everyday life.** Write connected sentences about personal details or daily life, using taught forms to make the intended meaning clear. `a1.writing.connected-description` — [§1.1.3, p. 8; §1.2.3, p. 9][a1-1]
- **Using information from a text.** Write a short response using the relevant facts from a simple source text without changing its essential information. `a1.writing.source-based-message` — [§1.2.3, p. 9][a1-1]

### Speaking

Evidence mode: independent speaking.

- **Speaking clearly enough to understand.** Produce taught words and short phrases with intelligible stress, consonant contrasts and phrase intonation; judge the audio itself. `a1.speaking.intelligibility` — [§2.1, p. 11; §1.2.4, p. 10][a1-1]
- **Greetings and polite exchanges.** Start or close a brief exchange and give thanks or an apology using address appropriate to the person. `a1.speaking.social-etiquette` — [§1.1.1, p. 7; §1.2.4 B, p. 10][a1-1]
- **Introducing yourself and another person.** Give a short connected introduction or description containing relevant personal or family information in original speech. `a1.speaking.personal-information` — [§1.1.3, p. 8; §1.2.4 A, p. 10][a1-1]
- **Asking and answering everyday questions.** Ask for needed information and answer a related follow-up about an item, place, time or personal detail. `a1.speaking.ask-and-answer` — [§1.1.1–1.1.2, p. 7; §1.2.4 B, p. 10][a1-1]
- **Requests, offers and replies.** Express a simple wish, request or invitation, then accept or decline in a way that fits the situation. `a1.speaking.request-and-response` — [§1.1.1, p. 7; §1.2.4 B, p. 10][a1-1]
- **Asking someone to repeat.** Signal that a spoken detail was not understood and ask for repetition instead of guessing an unrelated reply. `a1.speaking.repair` — [§1.1.1, p. 7; §1.2.4 B, p. 10][a1-1]
- **Retelling a short account.** Retell the main facts of a simple account in connected original speech, preserving who did what. `a1.speaking.simple-retelling` — [§1.2.4 A, p. 10][a1-1]

## A2

Includes the earlier A1 requirements.

### Language use

Evidence mode: contextual selection.

#### Nominative

- **What someone needs.** Choose the noun and agreeing form of нужен that name the item required. `a2.language.nominative-needed-item` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a2-1]
- **Naming a day or date.** Distinguish a calendar label from the inflected expression saying when an event happens. `a2.language.nominative-calendar-time` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a2-1]
- **What hurts or pleases someone.** Identify the grammatical subject in a familiar pain or preference construction, separately from the person experiencing it. `a2.language.nominative-state-cause` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a2-1]

#### Genitive

- **An amount of something.** Select the substance or item measured by a container or portion phrase. `a2.language.genitive-partitive` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a2-1]
- **Absence in the past or future.** Preserve an absence construction when moving a statement from now to a past or future situation. `a2.language.genitive-absence-time` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a2-1]
- **Many, few or several.** Choose the noun form after a taught indefinite quantity expression. `a2.language.genitive-indefinite-quantity` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a2-1]
- **When an event happened.** Use an event date including the appropriate day, month or year form, rather than merely naming today’s date. `a2.language.genitive-event-date` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a2-1]
- **Being at someone’s place.** Identify a person’s home or workplace as a location with у, distinguishing it from ownership. `a2.language.genitive-person-location` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a2-1]
- **Coming from a person.** Choose от with a person as the point of departure and contrast it with going to that person. `a2.language.genitive-person-origin` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a2-1]
- **Reaching a destination.** Use до to identify the endpoint or limit of a route without confusing it with the starting point. `a2.language.genitive-route-limit` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a2-1]
- **Before, during and after.** Place an event relative to another event by choosing a taught temporal preposition and noun form. `a2.language.genitive-time-relations` — [§2.2.2, p. 12][a2-1]

#### Dative

- **The age of a named person.** Express the age of a person identified by a noun phrase, with its dependent words agreeing. `a2.language.dative-age-named-person` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a2-1]
- **Who needs to act.** Identify a named person as the one who needs to act, rather than using a nominative actor by analogy. `a2.language.dative-necessity-named-person` — [§2.2.2, pp. 12–13][a2-1]
- **Who feels or likes something.** Select the person experiencing a feeling, condition or preference in a dative construction. `a2.language.dative-experiencer` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a2-1]
- **Moving along a route.** Use по with the area or route of movement, distinguishing it from entering a destination. `a2.language.dative-route-po` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a2-1]
- **A means of communication.** Choose a familiar medium expression with по for receiving or communicating information. `a2.language.dative-medium-po` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a2-1]
- **The subject of study.** Choose a subject label after по in a taught educational noun phrase. `a2.language.dative-subject-po` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a2-1]

#### Accusative

- **How often something happens.** Distinguish a recurring time phrase from duration and a single scheduled date. `a2.language.accusative-recurrence` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a2-1]

#### Instrumental

- **Working as or becoming someone.** Select a profession or role complement with работать, стать or a past form of быть. `a2.language.instrumental-role-change` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a2-1]
- **Being interested in something.** Choose the dependent noun phrase required by интересоваться in a statement about interests. `a2.language.instrumental-interest` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a2-1]
- **Above, below and beside.** Choose a stationary spatial relation and the governed noun form from a clearly described scene. `a2.language.instrumental-spatial-relations` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a2-1]

#### Prepositional

- **In a month or during a week.** Select a taught month or week expression with its correct preposition and agreeing modifiers. `a2.language.prepositional-calendar-time` — [§2.2.2, p. 13][a2-1]

#### Adjectives

- **Adjectives across cases.** Choose an adjective ending that matches the noun’s gender, number and case in an oblique phrase. `a2.language.adjective-case-agreement` — [§2.2.4, p. 14][a2-1]
- **Comparing familiar qualities.** Use a taught comparative form to express the intended difference between people, objects or places. `a2.language.adjective-comparison` — [§2.2.4, p. 14][a2-1]

#### Pronouns

- **The subject’s own possession.** Distinguish what belongs to the clause’s subject from what belongs to another participant. `a2.language.possessive-svoj` — [§2.2.3, p. 14][a2-1]
- **Each, all and self.** Select a taught determiner and its inflected form so the intended group, individual or independent action remains clear. `a2.language.determiner-reference` — [§2.2.3, p. 14][a2-1]
- **Pronoun agreement across cases.** Choose the case form of a familiar pronoun required by its role; maintain agreement where it modifies a noun. `a2.language.pronoun-oblique-agreement` — [§2.2.3, p. 14][a2-1]

#### Verbs of motion

- **Flying now or regularly.** Distinguish one directed flight from habitual or non-directed flying using an explicit time and route context. `a2.language.motion-flying` — [§2.2.5, p. 14][a2-1]
- **Carrying on foot.** Distinguish a directed act of carrying from habitual or non-directed carrying; make the carrier and carried object clear. `a2.language.motion-carrying` — [§2.2.5, p. 14][a2-1]
- **Transporting by vehicle.** Choose a conveying verb when a person transports someone or something by vehicle, distinguishing it from their own travel. `a2.language.motion-conveying` — [§2.2.5, p. 14][a2-1]
- **Entering and leaving a space.** Use taught в- or вы- motion forms to identify crossing into or out of a bounded space. `a2.language.motion-enter-exit` — [§2.2.5, p. 14][a2-1]
- **Leaving a place.** Use a taught у- form for departure from a place, distinguishing that departure from merely stepping outside. `a2.language.motion-leaving` — [§2.2.5, p. 14][a2-1]
- **Movement and its result.** Select a taught motion form that fits direction, means of travel and whether the movement is recurring, in progress or completed. `a2.language.motion-aspect-context` — [§2.2.5, p. 14][a2-1]

#### Verb forms

- **Action on someone or mutual action.** Distinguish a taught transitive verb from its reflexive counterpart and adjust the participant phrase accordingly. `a2.language.reflexive-complement` — [§2.2.5, p. 14][a2-1]
- **Frequent irregular verb forms.** Choose taught present or future forms of frequent verbs with less regular stems, including eating, drinking and singing. `a2.language.expanded-verb-forms` — [§2.2.5, p. 14][a2-1]
- **Duration, repetition and result.** Choose aspect in a short sequence where duration, repeated experience, an action phase or a completed result determines the meaning. `a2.language.aspect-phase-and-result` — [§2.2.5, p. 14][a2-1]

#### Numbers

- **Ordinal numbers in phrases.** Choose an ordinal form that agrees with a noun or date in the required case. `a2.language.ordinal-declension` — [§2.2.6, p. 14][a2-1]

#### Word formation

- **People and actions from word families.** Recognise taught derivations for occupations, nationality, female persons or actions while retaining the new word’s own grammatical identity. `a2.language.noun-derivation` — [§2.2.1, p. 11][a2-1]
- **Related adjectives and adverbs.** Select a taught adjective or adverb derived from a familiar word to fit its role in the sentence. `a2.language.adjective-adverb-derivation` — [§2.2.1, p. 11][a2-1]
- **Recognising verb families.** Recognise taught prefix and suffix changes that alter a verb’s aspect or motion meaning; do not assume every prefixed form is interchangeable. `a2.language.verb-derivation` — [§2.2.1, p. 11][a2-1]

#### Particles

- **Questions and negative meaning.** Interpret taught particles that distinguish a neutral question, expectation, surprise or negation in a short exchange. `a2.language.question-and-negative-particles` — [§2.2.8, p. 15][a2-1]

#### Prepositions

- **In a stated amount of time.** Distinguish a future time offset with через from an action’s duration or a past time reference. `a2.language.future-time-offset` — [§2.2.8, p. 15][a2-1]

#### Sentence structure

- **If one thing happens.** Choose an outcome consistent with an explicit condition and connect the clauses without reversing the relationship. `a2.language.conditional-clause` — [§2.3.5, p. 17][a2-1]
- **Explaining a purpose.** Distinguish what an action is intended to achieve from what caused it, using a taught purpose construction. `a2.language.purpose-clause` — [§2.3.5, p. 17][a2-1]
- **Reason and consequence.** Choose between a reason and a consequence connector while preserving the direction of explanation. `a2.language.cause-consequence` — [§2.3.5, p. 17][a2-1]
- **Reporting questions and requests.** Select an indirect report that preserves whether someone asked for information or asked another person to act. `a2.language.indirect-questions-requests` — [§2.3.6, p. 17][a2-1]
- **A noun inside a relative clause.** Choose a taught relative-pronoun form from its role inside the relative clause, while preserving the antecedent’s gender and number. `a2.language.relative-clause-agreement` — [§2.3.5, p. 17][a2-1]
- **An action without a named actor.** Interpret a familiar action stated without a named actor without falsely assigning it to a specific person. `a2.language.indefinite-personal` — [§2.3.1, p. 15][a2-1]
- **What the sentence emphasises.** Match an ordinary word-order choice to the question being answered; do not reject an alternative solely for differing from English order. `a2.language.information-structure` — [§2.3.4, p. 16][a2-1]

### Reading

Evidence mode: reading selection.

- **Main ideas and supporting details.** Identify a text’s main point and locate the supporting detail needed for a practical decision, without treating every sentence as equally important. `a2.reading.gist-and-detail` — [§1.2.2, p. 9][a2-1]
- **Finding a specific answer.** Select relevant information from a longer everyday text or several short messages according to a stated purpose. `a2.reading.strategy` — [§1.2.2, p. 9][a2-1]
- **Following a connected account.** Track people, times, causes and outcomes across a connected account rather than matching a single repeated word. `a2.reading.event-relations` — [§1.2.2, p. 9][a2-1]
- **Acting on a written message.** Choose an appropriate next action after reading an everyday notice, instruction or personal message. `a2.reading.functional-response` — [§1.1.2, p. 7; §1.2.2, p. 9][a2-1]

### Listening

Evidence mode: listening selection.

- **Understanding a longer spoken account.** Recover the main point and requested supporting details from a connected recording without a transcript revealing them. `a2.listening.monologue-details` — [§1.2.1 A, p. 8][a2-1]
